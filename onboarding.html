Redesigning Banco Next’s onboarding experience to meet the needs of younger generations and those with variable incomes.

Overview

Consultancy
Capco
Client
Bradesco (Next Bank)
Role
Product Design & Strategy, UX Research

Bradesco launched Next bank to provide digital banking services for younger generations of Brazilians. R/GA created a brand identity and design language for their digital products, but Next bank found that the account registration rate was very low: Out of 100 people who began onboarding, only 13 were able to finish the process. We spent 6 weeks conducting lean research to understand primary pain points, and worked on-site with the client to propose a more effective workflow.

The existing flow

Upon reviewing the flow, we knew that the redesign needed to guide users to appropriate account plans by making information accessible, removing redundancies where possible, and building trust with professional tone & clear language.

User research insights

After conducting a 2-day onsite research study, we uncovered 4 main pain points:

Lack of incentive

Users don’t see the value in being customers

Anxiety-inducing

Users feel anxious and uncomfortable giving up their personal information

Poor error handling

Users struggled to understand the errors they made and how to fix it

No process transparency

Users were fatigued by the length of the process

Persona development

In collaboration with the client’s anthropologist, we combined our research insights to craft distinct personas for our stakeholders to reference:

Paulo

Financially Independent

“If this bank is going to be a part of my life, there shouldn’t be so many limitations.”

Tech consideration
→ Has a fancier phone (iPhone)
→ Unlimited data plan

Luiza

Financially Dependent

“Opening a bank account via app should be easier than the traditional approach.”

Tech consideration
→ Has an older android phone
→ Relies on free-wifi/limited data plan

Sergio

Self-employed/Freelancer

“Why does the bank need all of this information from me? I don’t feel comfortable.”

Tech consideration
→ Has an older android phone (Oreo OS)
→ Relies on free-wifi/limited data plan

Our approach

By having a clear idea of what the problems were and who our user types were, we focused on addressing every issue in our redesign. We stayed in São Paulo for 2 weeks so that we could meet with various departments to understand their experience, and to workshop solutions together with our research insights and user types in mind.

Demonstrate product value immediately

Instead of demanding the user to submit all personal information & documentation, we only ask for their phone # (ID verification), and their Brazilian tax ID.

Users can then see account & card plans they can get, which is a strong incentive for conversion and reduces cognitive load for users who have low-intermediate financial literacy.

Leverage OCR technology

After speaking with engineers, we proposed using the bank’s OCR reading technology to read and auto-complete relevant data fields. This allows users to skip manual entry for the following screens:

Auto-completing 9 inputs

saves ~10-15min of time and prevents potential errors

Simplifying the tutorials

The onboarding process contained several multi-screen tutorials (below). In testing, we noticed that most users barely read the screens, swiping as quickly as possible. We saw an opportunity to improve the experience by simplifying the tutorial to one prompt, and providing contextual help.

Tone matters

Feedback from users and local team members revealed that the tone of voice felt outdated and corny, which impacted their trust in the product (i.e. “Agora vai lá ;)”)

“It’s like the equivalent of your dad saying ‘That’s groovy’”

Make terms & conditions accessible

Since “fees” were the biggest anxiety across our personas, we prototyped an example of how the terms & conditions could be more accessible to them.

A large search field with relevant tags should help users hone in on policies they care about, like fees & data privacy.

Final flow

We presented the final flow with our recommendations to the client and the work was well-received. We reduced the number of screens, gave users a strong incentive to convert, and provided clarity and professionalism where possible.

Next steps
